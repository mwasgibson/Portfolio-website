My research interests connect machine learning with practical problems in public health and cybersecurity. I am interested in how useful signals can be extracted from noisy data, how models should be evaluated, and how systems can communicate uncertainty rather than hide it.

## Disease-outbreak signals from social media

This line of work explores whether public social-media content can provide complementary signals for public-health monitoring. The central challenge is distinguishing potentially useful patterns from noise, misinformation, changing platform behaviour, and ordinary seasonal variation.

Areas of investigation include:

- Text processing and classification for symptom-related language.
- Time- and location-aware analysis of unusual changes in signal frequency.
- Model evaluation, false-positive analysis, and the limits of social-media data.
- Treating outputs as complementary signals, not a replacement for clinical or official surveillance.

The related [EpiPredict Kenya AI project](https://github.com/mwasgibson/epi-predict-kenya-ai) provides a software context for this interest.

## IoT DDoS detection and mitigation

This research area considers how network traffic analysis and anomaly detection can help identify denial-of-service patterns affecting resource-constrained connected devices.

Key considerations include:

- Establishing representative baselines for normal device traffic.
- Identifying traffic patterns associated with abnormal or malicious activity.
- Considering lightweight detection approaches suitable for constrained environments.
- Evaluating detection quality, resource cost, and the consequences of false alarms.

## Research practice

I aim to keep research claims proportional to the evidence available. A proposed method, prototype, experiment, and validated result are different things. Documentation should make those distinctions clear, describe limitations, and provide enough methodological detail for meaningful review.
